# Sample SOP: social services grievance handling

This is a sample standard operating procedure written for the Embrace demo. It follows common grievance redress practice in social protection programmes (cash transfers, food aid, disability and elderly support), but it is not any real agency's SOP. Replace it with your agency's own document.

## 1. Receiving a grievance

Grievances are accepted from any channel: web form, messaging apps, email, phone, social media and walk-in at the office or a community worker. Every grievance gets a ticket number and an acknowledgement within 1 working day. The acknowledgement states the ticket number, the team that will handle it and the expected first response time. Beneficiaries never pay any fee to file a grievance.

## 2. Emergencies

A household is in crisis when it has no food today or nowhere to sleep tonight, when someone is being beaten or thrown out of their home, or when someone talks about suicide. These cases are alerted to the head of the Protection Unit at once and receive a first response within 2 hours. The officer may arrange emergency food support or temporary shelter while the underlying problem is solved. Late or missing aid alone is not an emergency; it is rated high or normal.

## 3. Benefit not received or late

When a payment has not arrived, the officer checks within 72 hours: (a) whether the person is on the current beneficiary list, (b) the payment status for this period, and (c) whether the card or account is active. If the payment failed for a technical reason, the officer files a correction with the payment agency and tells the beneficiary the new expected date. If several beneficiaries in the same area report the same missing payment, the Benefits Team checks the batch as a whole.

## 4. Registration and eligibility

People who believe they are eligible but are not registered can ask for an assessment. The officer explains the documents needed (identity document, household card or proof of residence, and proof of income where required) and books a home visit by a community worker within 14 days. Eligibility decisions follow the programme rules; officers do not promise that a person will be accepted. Wrong data, such as a deceased member or a wrong address, is corrected in the registry within 14 days.

## 5. Payment schedule and pickup

Payment dates and pickup points are published each period. The officer gives the published schedule and the nearest pickup point. A beneficiary who cannot travel because of age, disability or illness can authorise a family member in writing to collect on their behalf.

## 6. Disability and elderly services

Requests for assistive devices, home care or a disability assessment go to the Social Rehabilitation Team. A first contact happens within 72 hours. Elderly people living alone without care are rated high and visited within 7 days.

## 7. Suspected fraud, illegal fees or deductions

Beneficiaries must receive their full entitlement. Any fee, cut, forced purchase or deduction by an agent, shop or official is prohibited. The Payment Oversight Team investigates reports within 7 days and protects the identity of the person reporting. Repeated reports about the same agent or shop trigger an audit of that agent.

## 8. Replying to the beneficiary

Replies are written in the beneficiary's language, in plain words. They acknowledge the specific problem, give the ticket number and the expected first response time in hours, and never promise that aid will be paid. Personal data such as identity numbers and card numbers is not repeated. Every reply drafted by software must be reviewed and approved by an officer before it is sent.

## 9. Closing a grievance

A grievance is closed when the problem is solved or a clear decision with reasons has been given, and the beneficiary has been informed. The beneficiary may appeal a decision within 30 days. Grievance statistics are reported to leadership every month, without personal data.

## 10. Disaster and emergency relief

During floods, fires and other disasters, reports of people trapped, injured or without shelter are emergencies: alert the supervisor at once, share the location with the disaster management agency and tell the reporter to call the local emergency number. Families at evacuation points must receive food, water, blankets and hygiene kits within 24 hours of registration. Households whose homes or goods were damaged can claim relief within 30 days: the officer records the address, the damage and a photo, and a field worker verifies within 7 days. Relief is paid to the household directly; no fee may be charged.
