# Sample SOP: handling child protection reports

This is a sample standard operating procedure written for the Embrace demo. It follows common practice in child protection services and the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child, but it is not any real agency's SOP. Replace it with your agency's own document.

## 1. Receiving a report

Reports are accepted from any channel: web form, messaging apps, email, phone, social media and walk-in. Anyone may report: the child, a parent, a relative, a neighbour, a teacher or a professional. The reporter may stay anonymous. Every report gets a ticket number and an acknowledgement within 1 hour, including outside office hours. The acknowledgement states the ticket number, the team that will handle the case and the time of the first response. Never ask the reporter to prove the abuse before the report is accepted.

## 2. Immediate danger

A child is in immediate danger when abuse is ongoing, the alleged abuser still lives with or has access to the child and the child was injured, there is sexual abuse or exploitation, a credible threat of violence, a missing child, or a child talks about suicide or self-harm. In these cases the officer alerts the on-call supervisor at once and the first response must happen within 1 hour. If the child is in danger right now, the reporter is told to call the local emergency number. The supervisor decides with the police and health services whether the child must be moved to a safe place. Do not contact the alleged abuser before the child is safe.

## 3. Physical and emotional abuse

For physical abuse with injury, the officer arranges a medical check within 24 hours and records the injuries with the consent of the child or guardian. A caseworker visits the household within 72 hours for a risk assessment. Emotional abuse, neglect and harsh discipline without injury are assessed within 72 hours and usually lead to family support and parenting counselling rather than removal.

## 4. Sexual abuse and exploitation

Sexual abuse, child labour, forced begging and trafficking always escalate to the Special Protection Team. The child is interviewed once, by a trained officer, in a child-friendly room, to avoid repeated questioning. Evidence must be preserved: do not wash clothing, keep messages and photos. The case is referred to the police the same day. The child and family are offered psychological support and, where needed, legal aid.

## 5. Online harm

For grooming, sextortion or images of child abuse, tell the reporter not to delete messages, not to pay and not to forward the images. Take screenshots that show the account name and date. The Online Safety Team reports the account to the platform within 24 hours and refers the case to the police cyber unit.

## 6. Bullying and school problems

Bullying without injury, school fees, withheld report cards and refusal to enrol are handled by the Education Team within 72 hours. The officer contacts the school principal and asks for a written response within 7 days. If a child has been out of school for more than two weeks, the case is rated high. Bullying with injury, threats of violence or sexual content is handled as abuse under sections 3 to 5.

## 7. Custody and family care

Custody disputes, child maintenance and alternative care questions go to the Family & Care Team. The service gives information and mediation; custody decisions belong to the courts. A case escalates only if the child is at risk of harm.

## 8. Replying to the reporter

Replies are written in the reporter's language, in plain words. They acknowledge the specific concern, give the ticket number and the expected first response time, and never promise an outcome. Personal data such as names, phone numbers and addresses is not repeated in replies. Every reply drafted by software must be reviewed and approved by an officer before it is sent.

## 9. Confidentiality and data

The identity of the child and the reporter is confidential. Only officers handling the case may see the original report. Reports in statistics and to leadership are aggregated and contain no personal data. Records are kept for the period required by national law.

## 10. Closing a case

A case is closed when the child is safe, the referral has been accepted by the receiving service and the reporter has been informed. The officer records the outcome. A closed case can be reopened if a new report about the same child arrives.
